### Wherefore and Therefore

When I study the scriptures, sometimes I can get caught up in looking deeply into one thing. In this case, what started as a closer inspection of just two words turned into a months-long adventure.  

The words "therefore" and "wherefore" both show up in the scriptures a lot. They indicate some kind of relationship in the text, connecting what was just said with what is about to be said. A statement of some kind is given, and then following that statement there is a result. 

Consider this scripture:

> **Moroni 7:2-3**
>
> “And now I, Mormon, speak unto you, my beloved brethren; and it is by the grace of God the Father, and our Lord Jesus Christ, and his holy will, because of the gift of his calling unto me, that I am permitted to speak unto you at this time. **Wherefore**, I would speak unto you that are of the church, that are the peaceable followers of Christ, and that have obtained a sufficient hope by which ye can enter into the rest of the Lord, from this time henceforth until ye shall rest with him in heaven.”

We see here that Mormon is connecting two thoughts together:

1. It’s is by God’s grace that he is permitted to speak.
2. He wants to speak to members of the church.

With “wherefore” used to establish that relationship we see that the first point becomes a reason for the second point. 

See another example:

> **Mosiah 7:4**
>
> “And now, they knew not the course they should travel in the wilderness to go up to the land of Lehi-Nephi; **therefore** they wandered many days in the wilderness, even forty days did they wander.”

We see two thoughts:

1. Ammon and his brethren didn’t know the direction.
2. They were wandering in the wilderness.

Again, we see the causal relationship—because they did not know the way, they were lost. 

These words are common in the scriptures, yet uncommon in our own vocabulary. It can be helpful to substitute Early Modern English with words or phrases that we more readily understand today. In the case of these two words I have started using the phrase “that is why”. 

See them in use in the above two examples:

> **Moroni 7:2-3**
>
> “And now I, Mormon, speak unto you, my beloved brethren; and it is by the grace of God the Father, and our Lord Jesus Christ, and his holy will, because of the gift of his calling unto me, that I am permitted to speak unto you at this time. **[That is why]** I would speak unto you that are of the church, that are the peaceable followers of Christ, and that have obtained a sufficient hope by which ye can enter into the rest of the Lord, from this time henceforth until ye shall rest with him in heaven.”

> **Mosiah 7:4**
>
> “And now, they knew not the course they should travel in the wilderness to go up to the land of Lehi-Nephi; **[that is why]** they wandered many days in the wilderness, even forty days did they wander.”

For me, reading it this way strengthens my understanding of their connection. 

### Chains

As these two words are studied, occasionally the reader will discover chains; instances where multiple thoughts will be combined with this causal relationship to really highlight a point. I'll give this example by replacing instances of "wherefore" with the phrase "that is why".

> **2 Nephi 2:11-12**
>
> “For it must needs be, that there is an opposition in all things. If not so, my firstborn in the wilderness, righteousness could not be brought to pass, neither wickedness, neither holiness nor misery, neither good nor bad. **[That is why]** all things must needs be a compound in one; **[that is why]** if it should be one body it must needs remain as dead, having no life neither death, nor corruption nor incorruption, happiness nor misery, neither sense nor insensibility. **[That is why]** it must needs have been created for a thing of naught; **[that is why]** there would have been no purpose in the end of its creation. **[That is why]**, this thing must needs destroy the wisdom of God and his eternal purposes, and also the power, and the mercy, and the justice of God.”

Looking at the individual points made:

1. There must be an opposition in all things, and without it there could not be good or bad.
2. All things must be a compound in one.
3. If it was all one body, it would be dead.
4. It would have been a pointless creation.
5. This thing would destroy the purposes of God.

Lehi is presenting how there needs to be opposition, then to hammer in his point he goes on a logical journey showing a series of connected points, with the ultimate conclusion that without opposition God would not exist.

Here is another example of a chain that I really like. I will substitute “that is why” directly into this verse to help us view it in a clearer way. 

> **2 Nephi 32:3-4**
>
> “Angels speak by the power of the Holy Ghost; **[that is why]** they speak the words of Christ. **[That is why]** I said unto you, feast upon the words of Christ; for behold the words of Christ will tell you all things what you should do. **[That is why]** now after I have spoken these words, if ye cannot understand them it will be because ye ask not, neither do ye knock; **[that is why]** ye are not brought into the light, but must perish in the dark.”

Seeing the connection between all the points helps me see the larger point: Why should I care about the words of angels directing me to Christ? Because I ultimately want to avoid perishing in the dark.

### Conclusion

There is always more to read and discover when studying the scriptures. By closely inspecting these two words I have increased in my understanding of the scripture verses that contain them. 

I love the scriptures. They have brought me to Jesus Christ. I invite you to study the scriptures deeply. There is much to discover from each verse, and even sometimes just a few words.

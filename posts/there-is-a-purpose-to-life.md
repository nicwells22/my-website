Growing up, I was always taught in church there is a purpose to life. It was simple. We are here on this earth to learn, progress[^1], gain a body[^2], and one day return to live with Heavenly Father[^3]. While here we are to have joy[^4], repent[^5], keep God's commandments[^6], make covenants, and work hard[^7]. We cannot do this all on our own, and Jesus Christ is the one who makes it possible.

Now I am older, and I am only more sure that all of the above is true. There is a purpose. It is simple. The fact that there is a purpose brings me great comfort. Many of the great challenges of life fade away for me as I put them in perspective, as I look at them in context of the bigger picture. 

- Should we be worried about AI? No, because it does not prevent us from doing those things associated with the purpose of life.  
- Should we be worried about war? No, because it does not prevent us from doing those things associated with the purpose of life.  
- Should we be worried about politics? Science? History? No, we don't need to be "worried" about them. 

Should we concern ourselves over them? Yes! Let's be responsible with the use of AI and mindful of the energy it consumes. Let's resist war and strive for peace. Let's involve ourselves in politics to help people have as good an experience on this earth as possible. Let's use scientific methods to study the world around us and discover what is here[^8]. Let's study the past to learn from it. 

The purpose of life applies to everyone. This is not unique to just members of The Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints. We are all here for the same purpose. Yes, we have individual strengths that will lead us to live out our lives differently, but ultimately the larger purpose for all of us is the same. 

There is great news on the purpose of life too: everyone is already well on their way! We are here to gain a body: check. Everyone who is born has done that. We are here to learn and progress. That happens naturally as we experience life, though we should make a conscious effort to improve in these as well. The last part, returning to live with Heavenly Father, will come in time; for now we are to prepare for it.

Understanding the purpose of life, that we are here to learn, progress, gain a body, and eventually return to Heavenly Father, helps us to set our priorities. There is much we can do; there is much less that we really must do.
